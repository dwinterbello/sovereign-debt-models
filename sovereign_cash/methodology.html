1. One chain, four horizons

What is forecast at each horizon

The debt board, the cash boards and the shock dashboard share one chain. Each horizon has one model: cash/share/DTS where the walk-forward test runs (h≤3), WEO primary beyond (the US line model to FY2030). Scenarios run one line model for growth, inflation and rates (Section 10). Interest stays on the ISIN book. The table below lists the handoffs; the rest of this page derives each row.

p is a period (calendar year, or fiscal year for the UK and US); m a month; T the last complete outturn period. D̂p is the forecast primary deficit (+ = deficit). Ĉm is the monthly cash requirement from the country cash model (Section 11 onward), built line by line where the country publishes lines. r̂p and q̂p are the receipts and primary-spending shares carried forward from T (Section 3), Yp nominal GDP and Xp the price level (Section 2). PolQp and PolRp are legislated spending and tax measures, static authority costings net of the base year (one section covers them, Section 5) — applied only inside the tested window. Beyond T+3 the GG primary is the IMF WEO primary deficit πWEO_p (Section 6), except the US, whose line model (Section 17) runs to FY2030; policy overlays are dropped there because WEO already embeds them. Interest, debt and issuance follow from D̂p through the ISIN book (Section 7 to Section 9) — WEO interest is never an input.

Nominal GDP and the price level

House CPI is the price driver through 2030; WEO supplies real growth and the beyond-2030 / fail fallback.

Fiscal-year labelling

The UK and US publish fiscal-year borrowing. Their macro drivers are calendar series, so each is moved to the fiscal year by month count before any share is formed.

3. Receipts and primary spending as shares, T+1 to T+3

Receipts to GDP, spending to prices

Beyond the cash window the engine does not forecast cash lines; it forecasts two shares and multiplies by the macro path. A share is a stationary object a walk-forward test can grade, a cash level is not.

Rp is receipts and Qp primary spending in the country's borrowing measure (federal deficit for the US; PSNB ex banks for the UK; general-government deficit for ES/SE/NO/JP; for FR/DE/IT the same measure as their cash boards). rp is the receipts share of nominal GDP and qp primary spending relative to the price level, so spending is real in the no-policy case; Sweden's primary is the one line carried as a share of GDP (persist-GDP), where the test chose it. R̂, Q̂ for h = 1…3 are the carried shares times the macro path of Section 2. Origins since 2005; Covid years 2020–21 are excluded from fitting and scoring. The walk-forward test is conditional on realised GDP and prices at each origin (macro path known; share rule is what is scored).

Persist or revert

Two carry rules only. Anything richer (trend, cyclical adjustment, authority path) failed the walk-forward test or cannot be tested on twenty annual origins.

rT is the share at the last outturn, h the horizon in periods. Under persist the share is held. Under revert it decays to the sample mean μ (years 2020–21 left out) at annual persistence ρ, the no-intercept AR(1) slope of the demeaned share, clipped to [0, 1] and set to 1 when fewer than five year pairs exist. The same two rules apply to q with the price-level share.

Which rule ships, per country and line

The choice is made on the level error of the line, not on the headline deficit: a share rule that wins the headline by cancelling another line's error is not evidence for the rule.

The walk-forward test refits from every origin year o from 2005 to T−1 and scores the line level L (receipts or primary spending, local bn) one to three years out given realised GDP and prices at o; target years in 2020–21 are dropped. Revert replaces persist only if its mean MAE over h = 1…3 is more than REVERTGAIN = 10% better. The headline share MAE on the Methodology page is therefore conditional on realised macro; the companion score under point-in-time WEO vintages is ``derived/ownannual_vintagescore.json`` (UK/US/ES/SE/NO/JP — the own-annual set; FR/DE/IT use component models, not this file).

4. UK receipts: frozen thresholds

Income tax and NICs under frozen thresholds

A frozen personal allowance makes income tax grow faster than earnings. Persist on a share of GDP misses that by construction, so the UK receipts line is the one place the chain carries a legislated elasticity instead of a carried share. Backtest: freeze-era bias −0.97% of GDP under persist, −0.76% under this rule; on freeze-era and full-sample MAE it loses to persist (34.0 vs 31.5 £bn; 0.713 vs 0.688% of GDP) and ships as a structural prior, not on the gate.

sT is the income-tax-plus-NICs share of receipts at the origin (OBR authority file; 0.435 fallback), Et average earnings on the fiscal-year basis, Yt nominal GDP. Through FY2027-28 — the last year the Autumn Statement 2022 freeze would have run under prior law — IT+NICs compound with earnings at elasticity ε = 1.3 (OBR EFO October 2026 ¶4.14: +1pp earnings ≈ £7bn under frozen thresholds against ≈ £4.5bn if CPI-indexed, ratio 4.5/7 = 0.84 for the indexed counterfactual). After that the elevated level is unit-elastic to GDP, so the Budget 2025 extension enters only through the policy line. The rest of receipts is unit-elastic to GDP.

5. Primary deficit, legislated policy and the general-government wedge

Primary deficit with legislated policy

Shares give the no-policy path; policy is added on top as the authorities costed it. Keeping the two apart is what lets the authority bridge of the shock-dashboard bridge (Equations) say how much of a gap to the OBR or CBO is macro and how much is measures.

Dp is the primary deficit in the country's measure (+ = deficit). PolQp and PolRp are the authority's static costings of measures legislated by the origin, summed by line and expressed net of the base year, so a measure already in the T outturn is not counted twice; a positive tax measure raises receipts. In the US DTS years the tax measures of the 2025 reconciliation act are not added (USDTS_TAXOVERLAY = False): the Treasury cash levels already carry them, and CBO's FY26→27 tax increment is mostly the timing of its TCJA-expiry baseline; the spending measures (Medicaid and SNAP cuts phasing in after the base year) stay. The policy overlay — sources, signs, per-country event counts and its cross-checks against the authority — is one section, Section 5, shared with the shock dashboard.

From the measure to the general-government primary balance

6. After T+3: GG primary from WEO

WEO primary after the tested horizon

Short-horizon primary is ours; medium-term primary is the authority. The interest edge is the forward curve on the ISIN book, not a better primary path.

Beyond T+3 the GG primary deficit is the IMF WEO primary (rule weopct). The US is the exception: its DTS line model, every line on a driver (Section 17), runs to FY2030 (primary.USDTS_TOYEND), because handing FY2030 to WEO put a step of more than 1pp of GDP between FY2029 and FY2030 that no driver explains. The measure primary is set to πWEO−ω so that after the held GG wedge (Section 5) the stamped GG primary equals WEO; ω=0 where the measure is already GG. Share / cash / DTS rules are tested only one to three years out (TESTEDH = 3); freezing the T+3 residual (heldpct) was an unscored convenience that manufactured artefacts (Japan P-driver surplus) and dressed authority consolidation gaps as house views. heldpct remains only if a WEO cell is missing. Policy overlays are zeroed in those years — WEO already includes them. Japan still forces primary spend onto GDP inside the tested window (PERSISTGDP_PRIMARY) so the handoff year is not poisoned by Y−P drift. Interest stays on the ISIN book. Horizon 2030 (YEND).

7. Interest from the ISIN book

Interest from the ISIN book

Interest is not a ratio times a stock: every bond in the book is rolled month by month, with locked coupons on the existing stock and the curve only on what has to be issued. The yield shock channels of the shock part (Section 7) change y and ref and nothing else.

Im is cash interest in month m. For every ISIN i in the book: N the outstanding nominal (less the central bank's holding where the measure is private), c the coupon, payi the payment months. Floaters reset on the reference rate refm plus spread sj; bills pay their discount 1 − Pk at maturity; index-linked coupons scale with the CPI ratio at the issue's lag (RPI for gilts). New issues n needed to fund the cash requirement are priced at the forward par yield y(mn, τn) of the government zero curve at their issue month and tenor (next equation), tenor mix from the trailing year's issuance. ĪNM is the non-marketable residual (retail, loans, intra-government) frozen at its last outturn.

Forward par yield on the government zero curve

Issuing at the forward par yield is the arbitrage-free way to book a coupon that does not yet exist; it means the interest path moves with the whole curve, not with one headline yield.

P(0, t) is today's discount factor from the fitted government zero curve (Nelson–Siegel–Svensson on the country's benchmark yields); P(m, k) the forward discount factor for a k-year cash flow issued at m. y(m, τ) is the par coupon that prices a τ-year bond at 100 when issued in month m — annual coupons, so the formula is annual. Beyond the last liquid tenor the curve is held flat.

8. Net issuance and the financing wedge

Net issuance

Net issuance is an identity on the pieces already derived; the only new input is the financing wedge, which no model in this chain claims to forecast.

NIp is net marketable issuance; Dp the primary deficit and Ip interest from the sections above; Wp the financing wedge — the part of the published financing requirement outside the deficit (lending, financial transactions, cash-buffer change, FX and timing), carried from the authority's financing table where there is one and from its trailing outturn otherwise. ΔHCBp is the central bank's net purchase (negative under run-off), so NIpriv is what the private sector must take. Gross issuance adds redemptions from the ISIN book's maturity calendar and announced buybacks.

How fast the effective rate reaches the marginal rate

A rate shock reaches the interest bill only as the book turns over. Reading ρ off the ISIN book, not a stylised average maturity, is what makes the US and Italy diverge in the fan.

rm is today's marginal rate — the ISIN book's issuance-weighted yield on the government curve — and Δ the scenario shock in rate terms. s3y is the share of the stock that reprices within three years, from the book's maturity and floater schedule, so ρ is the equivalent constant annual repricing fraction. r0 is GG interest to GDP divided by debt to GDP in the last complete outturn year on the debt panel (not the forecast-year row).

10. Scenarios: one line model for growth, inflation and rates

Every scenario leg on the same budget lines

One model carries growth, inflation and rates, so a downturn, a reflation and a sell-off differ only in which drivers move, not in which model answers. The shock dashboard (Equations) still runs the aggregate channels.

ΔDc,y is the change in country c's primary deficit in year y against baseline (+ = more borrowing), the same function for every scenario in the note's grid, the repression chart and the downturn line of the scenario-interest chart. A nominal-growth shock of g pp a year for two years (level gap g, then 2g, no catch-up) splits into a real part (REALSHARE = 0.75; Section 10) and a price part xy = 0.25 × gap on the price leg; an inflation shock is the price leg alone with xy the price-level gap; a rates shock moves only the receipts that rates move. ℓ are budget lines: US federal Daily Treasury Statement buckets, UK OBR ready-reckoner lines, elsewhere personal income tax, social contributions, corporate tax, indirect taxes and current primary spending. Interest ΔI: the ISIN book re-run on the shifted curve (calendar years through 2030 for every country, straight-line between the −100 / 0 / +100bp runs), the marginal rate rm on the extra debt, and index-linked debt L accruing the extra inflation at once. US general government: state and local receipts move with nominal GDP and their spending matches them (balanced budgets), so only the interest / revenue denominator moves. 'Half spent' adds half of an upturn's receipts windfall to spending. Baseline interest: the debt board's path, scaled to the reviewed interest / revenue in 2026 and 2030.

The price leg, common to growth and inflation

Inflation helps the primary for a year or two because receipts reprice before spending does, while linkers cost at once; the same leg answers the price part of every growth scenario, so reflation and nominal growth cannot disagree.

xy is the price-level gap: 0.25 × the nominal gap in a growth scenario, the inflation path (infl, then 2 × infl) in reflation. Receipts follow their nominal bases in the year. Primary spending follows a year later (benefit and pension uprating on last year's inflation, pay settlements and budgets set ahead) for the share π that is price-linked within the horizon. π = 0.8 is calibrated to CBO's inflation rule of thumb (outlays 44 / 95 / 156bn per pp in years 2-4 against ~62bn a year for full lagged indexation) and fits the EU spending mix; a test holds the US price leg within a factor of two of CBO each year. US lines take the same rule line by line. The UK runs the OBR reckoner's price determinants (CPI, RPI, earnings, consumption, profits, GDP, imports, house prices; September CPI uprating, the triple lock, frozen thresholds) and, because the reckoner holds departmental spending (DEL, ~half of primary spending) fixed in cash, adds DEL at the same π a year later. In a growth scenario, lines already on nominal paths (US corporate and non-withheld tax, UK profits, corporate tax elsewhere) are left out of the price leg. Index-linked debt L (US, UK, France: the issuer's stock with uplift; Germany, Italy, Spain, Japan: the ISIN tape's linker face) accrues the higher inflation at once, as interest. Fiscal drag from unindexed thresholds in IT, ES and JP and Japan's pension macro slide are not modelled.

The trailing-level model family

France, Germany, Italy: component models

Each driven line is a share of a macro driver, and the share is forecast by a seasonal time-series model. Figure 3 shows the shares carried forward; the specification choice, fitted coefficients and walk-forward tests follow it.

c indexes the driven lines and t months. yc,t is the line's cash flow and Xc,t its driver's level, lagged two months: receipts on nominal GDP (seasonally adjusted, interpolated from quarters), primary spending on the HICP index, Italy's capital spending and other revenue on nominal GDP. ωc,t is the line's share of that level. France, Germany, Italy: why a shock to the level is exact

Because the share forecast ignores the driver, a shock to the driver's level moves the line in proportion and needs no refit. Figure 7 shows France's receipts: a one-off shift persists and growth compounds.

ŷc,m is the model's baseline forecast of line c in month m and ω̂c,m the share forecast behind it, so ŷ = ω̂ X̂ with X̂ the baseline driver level. 32. Our forecast against each authority's

Our forecast against the authority's: the bridge

The bridge splits every bn between our forecast and the authority's into a definitional part and a forecast part, line by line. Figure 13 stacks the parts; the diamonds are the total gap.

k runs over the three lines receipts, primary spending and interest, with σk as in Section 22. Boursp is our forecast with legislated policy (Section 31), Bauthp the budgetary authority's own total for the same period, L̂k,p our line k and Ak,p the authority's, split on the authority's published lines. Lines are not always defined identically (our UK and US interest is gross, the authorities' net), so each line's difference is split into dk, the difference already present in the last outturn year 0 (our outturn less the authority's), grown with nominal GDP Yp/Y0, and the forecast difference fk,p left after it. The pieces add to the total gap exactly. France, Germany and Italy are compared on the total only: their plans have no line split we can match.

A yield shock reprices every bond issued after it, so interest is computed from the bond-by-bond book at fixed shifts and read between them. Figure 14 shows interest against the shift, one line per year.

Ip is interest in period p given a parallel shift Δr (bp) of the government zero curve. i indexes bonds already outstanding, with locked coupon ci and notional Ni,p outstanding in p; k indexes new issues, with notional Nk,p, priced at the forward par yield fk(Δr) off the zero curve shifted by Δr. The book is run at −200, −100, 0, +100, +200, +300bp. For a slider value s (bp) between two runs ra ≤ s ≤ rb, ΔIp(s) is interpolated in a straight line from the changes ΔIp(ra) and ΔIp(rb) against the 0bp run. How a shift at one maturity spreads across the curve is the next section's equation.

34. Bond yields: the shape of the curve

Bond yields: the 3-month and 10-year points

A shock at one point has to move the rest of the curve, and the conditional expectation from history does it. Figure 15 shows the curve that follows a 3-month-only and a 10-year-only move.

u is a maturity and δ(u) the change in its zero rate (bp). x3m and x10y are the moves in the two anchor points that the user sets. Σ is the covariance of monthly changes in zero rates since 2000, the matrix the principal components come from: Σu,a holds each maturity's covariances with the anchors and Σa,a the anchors' own 2×2 covariance. Every maturity moves by its expected change given the two anchors: the anchors are hit exactly and level, slope and curvature all sit at their conditional means. The ISIN book (Section 33) is run on these shapes at −200…+300bp in each anchor; the two anchors' effects add.

35. Interest on the extra debt

Interest on the extra debt

Extra borrowing is itself financed at the marginal new-issue rate, and that interest is borrowed again. Figure 16 is the same shock with feedback off and on; the gap between the bars is F.

t is a forecast period (one year). Dt is the scenario's own change in borrowing in t (the ΔB of Section 22 before F), St the cumulative extra debt at the end of t (S0 = 0) and Ft the interest on it. rt is the marginal new-issue rate in percent and Δr the yield shock in bp, so kt is the rate as a fraction. Debt carried in, St−1, pays the full year at kt; the year's new borrowing, including the feedback itself, pays half a year, which is why Ft appears on both sides and the denominator is 1 − kt/2.
